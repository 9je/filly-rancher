# Story

**Filly Rancher: a cozy ranch in a valley that runs on color.**

This is the complete v1 story. It is set in canon Equestria: Ponyville, Cloudsdale, the Everfree, and the show's own ponies, who appear as themselves (the roster of named characters the player can find, befriend and collect). The story's core cast below are original characters written for this game, living alongside them. No lyrics or audio from the fan song this game riffs on are used. The register is cheerful on the surface and dread underneath: the Company is funny the way an HR poster is funny, right up until it is not. Darkness is implied, never shown. Nothing gory is ever on screen; the worst things happen behind frosted glass, in paperwork, and in what is missing.

Every beat lists the **mechanic** it rides on. A beat with no mechanic is a cutscene, and this game has very few of those.

---

## 1. The world

### the Ponyville valley
A green valley ringed by soft mountains. A small town (Ponyville) of thatched cottages, a market, a post office and a schoolhouse with no school bell. Orchards, a mirror-flat lake, a meadow that blooms only at night, and a tangled wood at the far end. The light is warm, the grass is saturated green, and the sky always has a rainbow in it.

### The Rainbow
A permanent rainbow arcs over the valley from a palace of clouds on the northern ridge. The townsfolk love it. It is on the welcome sign, the postcards, the jam labels. **It is the factory's output, and it brightens every time a pony is delivered.**

### Cloudsdale Weather Corporation
"Cloudsdale Weather Corporation: Weather You Can Count On." Cloudsdale makes the valley's weather from the Rainbow Factory, the cloud palace on the ridge, and runs a charitable arm, the **Wayward Pony Program**. the Corporation's letters are relentlessly upbeat, carefully worded, and full of numbers. Its signature is "the Chairmare of Cloudsdale Weather". She is never seen.

### Drifters
The valley's wild ponies. Gentle, skittish, a little forgetful, and **blank-flanked**: none of them has a cutie mark. They wander in loose herds, graze, sleep in the grass, and spook at sudden movement. the Corporation's official line: Drifters are ponies who "lost their way" and need help finding their color. The Program pays stewards to gather them and send them up to "Placement at the Cloudsdale Academy", where they will "find their talents in the clouds."

**The truth** (never stated plainly until the reveal, and never fully explained even then): the Rainbow Factory distills a pony's color into spectrum. Only ponies who have not yet found their talent can be drained; a cutie mark means a pony's color is *hers*, fixed, and the machines cannot take it. the Corporation's gray rain, the "Cloudsdale Showers", makes ponies forget themselves, and some of them drift. The company farms the valley it waters.

### Blooming
A Drifter who is cared for (fed her favorite food, kept with ponies she likes, given things to do) grows a **Bloom meter**. When it fills, she **Blooms**: she earns her cutie mark (a generated mark that reflects a talent), stops being a Drifter, produces finer gems, and gets a name. This is the core long-term reward of ranching, and it is the single mechanic the whole story turns on.

Before the reveal, Blooming is just lovely. After it, Blooming is how you save a pony.

## 3. Act structure

The game runs on the world clock (one day is one 20-minute cycle; see GDD). Acts are gated by story flags raised by play, not by time alone. A typical player reaches the reveal in about 90 to 120 minutes.

### Act One: Sunny Acres (Days 1 to 3, about 60 minutes). Cozy, complete, good.

The first hour is a full game on its own: catch, pen, feed, Bloom, sell, buy, explore, meet the town. Nothing is visibly wrong. A careful player notices small details that are slightly off.

**Beat 1.1, Arrival.** A cart ride into the valley at dawn (the title-to-game transition). The rainbow is the first thing on screen. Marigold meets you at the ranch gate, hands you the Cloudsdale **Scoop** (the capture tool) and the keys, and leaves you with a cheerful line and a clipboard. *Mechanic: onboarding. The first Drifter is grazing ten meters from the gate, facing away.*

**Beat 1.2, Tumble.** On the first catch attempt a filly bounces into the beam and out again, giggling. Tumble attaches herself to you. She cannot be scooped ("too wiggly!"). From now on she follows you and stops to point her hoof at nearby Drifters (a sparkle over the Drifter). *Mechanic: companion and finder; teaches the beam by contrast.*

**Beat 1.3, First pen.** Old Fennel wanders over from next door, shows you the first plot, and talks you through feeding a Drifter her favorite food. *Mechanic: build pen, feed, needs. Fennel's request starts the Request Board.*

**Beat 1.4, First gems, first sale.** Penned and fed, the Drifter makes gems; Bramble buys them in town. *Mechanic: production, market. Bits fly to the counter.*

**Beat 1.5, The first Bloom.** About 20 minutes in, a well-fed pony Blooms: a little ceremony (she rears, sparkles, a mark appears on her flank, a fanfare, she gets a name and her talent). Tumble cheers. The pony's gems get finer. *Mechanic: Bloom. The first big reward.*

**Beat 1.6, The Depot.** Marigold explains the Program: Drifters you gather can be sent up for Placement at the Depot crate behind your barn. Cloudsdale pays the ranch a stipend for each. It is easily the best money available. **Bloomed ponies are politely refused** ("Oh, she's found herself already! Placement is for ponies still looking."). *Mechanic: delivery for bits; the ledger and the first collection day. Clue: why would an Academy refuse ponies who succeeded?*

**Beat 1.7, The town.** Pip brings your first letter (the Program's welcome pack, with a postcard). The town opens: market, post office, the board. *Mechanic: town hub, NPC talk, the Request Board.*

**Beat 1.8, Collection day one.** the Corporation's ledger asks for a small quota (two deliveries). You can meet it with Drifters you do not want to keep; the receipt arrives with a cheerful note. Out in the sky, the rainbow gets a little brighter, and a sparkle in the delivered pony's coat color flies up from the Depot and into it. It looks like a reward animation. *Mechanic: quota, delivery feedback. Clue (replay): it is the pony.*

### Act Two: Small Wrong Details (Days 3 to 6, about 45 minutes). Still cozy; the wrongness grows.

The loop deepens (new regions, rare Drifters, the casino, racing), and the clues come closer together. Every clue is optional to notice and is delivered through play, people or places, never through a narrator.

**Beat 2.1, Postcards.** After each delivery, a postcard arrives a day later: "Having a wonderful time at the Academy! I found my talent! Love, [name]." Every card has the same words and the same hoofwriting, whichever pony sent it. *Mechanic: mail. Clue.*

**Beat 2.2, Fennel's wall.** Visiting Fennel's cottage shows a wall of postcards from Clover, six years of them. "She writes every month. Never says much." They are the same card. *Mechanic: NPC home interior, talk. Clue.*

**Beat 2.3, Bramble's dye.** Bramble starts stocking **Cloudsdale Rainbow Dye**, "made with 100% real rainbow!" Each shade is named, and the names are the coat-color names of ponies the player has delivered ("Peach Sunrise No. 4"). *Mechanic: shop inventory driven by the delivery log. Strong replay clue.*

**Beat 2.4, Pip at the cloud line.** Pip mentions she is not allowed to fly above the cloud line, and that Academy mail is always bone dry, even in a storm. *Mechanic: NPC talk. Clue.*

**Beat 2.5, The Survey.** the Corporation's Employee Appreciation Survey: "Which of your ponies is most special to you?" Answering pays 50 bits and a coupon. If answered, a Priority Requisition for that pony arrives some days later, and a Collector comes for her at dawn. (If she has Bloomed by then, the Collector reads her flank, pauses, and leaves without her. The game never says why.) *Mechanic: letter with a reply; requisition; Bloom as protection. Clue: the first thing that hurts.*

**Beat 2.6, The Scoop's gauge.** The Scoop's little meter, labelled "Sparkle", reads higher for brighter-coated, happier ponies. A tooltip calls it "how much joy she's carrying!" *Mechanic: beam UI. Clue (relabelled after the reveal as "Spectral yield").*

**Beat 2.7, Night carts.** At night, cloud-carts float up the lift from the Depot to the Works, and the crates on them rock a little. *Mechanic: world clock and ambient set dressing. Clue.*

**Beat 2.8, Tumble and the Depot.** Tumble will not follow you within ten meters of the Depot. She sits and waits, ears down. *Mechanic: companion AI. Clue.*

**Beat 2.9, The bright ones.** the Corporation's requests on the board start asking for "vivid" Drifters, with bonuses for bright coats and rare colors. *Mechanic: request board. Clue.*

**Beat 2.10, Loading tips.** "Tip: Happy ponies make the brightest colors!" *Mechanic: loading screens and HUD greetings, rotating copy. Clue.*

**Beat 2.11, The invitation.** Once the player has made about eight deliveries and Bloomed at least three ponies, and the Act Two clues have had time to land, Marigold arrives with a gold-edged invitation. The ranch's performance has earned a spot at the **Color Day Open House** at the Rainbow Factory, tomorrow. The same day, Tumble's name is drawn in the **Placement Lottery** ("such an honor!"), and she is to go up on the same lift. Tumble hides under the porch. *Mechanic: story trigger on delivery and Bloom counts. The player cannot stop either event, and is not responsible for it: the Company did it (see RESEARCH.md on Spec Ops).*

### The Reveal: Color Day (Day 6, about 15 minutes). The set piece.

**R.1, The lift.** Dawn. The player and Tumble ride the cloud-lift up with a cart of crates. The music is the cozy theme, slightly too slow. The valley shrinks below; the rainbow grows enormous and roars like a waterfall.

**R.2, The tour.** The Rainbow Factory is pristine white cloud and gold trim, impossibly bright. Marigold guides a little group (the player and a few other stewards, all generated ponies) through the Welcome Hall (posters: "Every Pony Has a Color!"), the Academy Classroom (a classroom with no desks, painted on a wall), and the Gift Shop (Rainbow Dye). Each room is a little less saturated than the last. Tumble is led away to "orientation" by two Collectors. *Mechanic: walking, talking, environmental storytelling. The color grading begins to slide (dread rises from 0 to about 0.3).*

**R.3, Off the path.** A service door is left ajar. Tumble's little hoofprints in spilled color lead through it. The player follows: a narrow walkway above the intake floor, cloud turning to gray metal. Collectors walk below with lanterns; the player must move between their light cones (hold still in shadow when one turns). Being seen puts you back at the last door with a buzzer, not a fail state. *Mechanic: the light-cone stealth used later on the Rescue path, taught here in a safe space.*

**R.4, The window.** At the end of the walkway, a long frosted window above the processing floor. Behind it, silhouettes of ponies standing in a slow line. One steps into a ring of light. The light turns her coat color, then white, then a rainbow pours up a glass pipe and out of sight. A small gray shape is led away. The music drops to a single held note and a heartbeat. Through the glass, at the end of the line, the player sees a filly-sized silhouette with Tumble's tangerine coat. No gore, no screaming, no close-up: silhouettes, color, and sound. *Mechanic: the dread scalar goes to 0.8; full grading shift; the Scoop's gauge relabels in front of the player's eyes from "Sparkle" to "Spectral yield".*

**R.5, Marigold.** Marigold steps up beside you, smiling, not blinking. "Oh! You found the good part." She explains it plainly and cheerfully, in the Company's words: the valley needs weather, weather needs color, Drifters do not need theirs. Then she makes the offer.

**R.6, The choice.**
- **"Sign the Senior Steward contract."** Double stipend, Factory equipment, and, as "a little gesture", Tumble is struck from the lottery and goes home with you. "We look after our own." → **The Factory path.**
- **"Get Tumble out."** The player vaults the rail. A short, scripted, beam-driven escape: scoop Tumble out of the line (she is still too wiggly; it takes the full beam tug, the hardest catch in the game so far), then run the walkway back to the lift with Collectors' lanterns sweeping and an alarm rising, and drop the lift down to the valley. Marigold, at the window, just watches. → **The Rescue path.**

Both choices save Tumble. That is deliberate. The Factory path is not chosen by a monster; it is chosen by someone who just watched a machine and was offered the one pony they love back, plus money. The temptation is real.

The choice is final for the set piece, but not for the player's behavior afterward. Every delivery and every rescue afterward is a free choice, and the endings count them.

## 4. Act Three, the Factory path: "Senior Steward"

**Fantasy:** efficiency, rewards, and a valley going quietly gray. The path must be tempting: it is faster, richer, and full of new toys.

**What changes in play:**
- **Stipend doubled, upkeep waived.** The ledger's debt pressure vanishes. Income runs at roughly 2x the Rescue path.
- **Factory gear (new shop: the Cloudsdale Supply Desk at the Depot):**
  - **Calm Lamps** for pens: ponies under a lamp never flee, never get unhappy, and never Bloom. They also stop emoting: no ear flicks, no head turns, no happy hops. The pen goes quiet.
  - **Express Chute**: a pen upgrade that sends ponies to the Depot automatically at dawn, for a bonus. You never have to look at them again.
  - **Yield Lens** for the Scoop: shows a pony's spectral grade before you catch her. Vivid ponies pay three times as much.
  - **Lure Posts**: Drifters gather around them at night. Catching gets easy.
- **Contracts:** Cloudsdale posts contracts on the board ("Seven pastels by collection day, bonus 2,000"). Rank ups at contract milestones: Steward, Senior Steward, Supervisor, Regional Director. Each rank letter is warmer and mentions the yield.
- **The world responds:** each delivery after the reveal raises the **dread** scalar a little.
  - The valley desaturates, fog lingers, and the music thins to fewer instruments.
  - Town ponies stop appearing: the townsfolk pool shrinks and cottages get boarded windows.
  - Fennel stops answering his door.
  - Bramble's stall becomes a Cloudsdale franchise kiosk with better prices.
  - Pip delivers your mail without speaking.
  - Tumble lives at your ranch. She has stopped pointing at ponies. She sits on the porch facing the Works.
- **The tempting part:** the path is genuinely smoother, the rewards genuinely bigger, and the casino comps you. A player who just wants a big, efficient ranch will enjoy it, and feel it.

**Factory climax: the Jubilee.** At Regional Director, Cloudsdale commissions the **Jubilee Rainbow**: deliver a final grand order (a set number of ponies including at least a few vivid ones), then ride up to the Works for the Jubilee and **pull the lever** that starts the Grand Spectrum yourself, on a stage, to applause. The rainbow at the end is the most beautiful thing in the game.

## 5. Act Three, the Rescue path: "The Lantern Line"

**Fantasy:** using everything you have learned, to save ponies under pressure. Harder, riskier, poorer, and right.

**Setup:** you land at the bottom of the lift with Tumble and a Cloudsdale alarm sounding. That night **Static** is waiting at your fence. She explains what she can and offers you a way to help: the **Lantern Line**, a hidden path out of the valley through the tangled wood, where marked ponies can live free.

**What changes in play:**
- **The ledger turns on you.** No stipend, upkeep rises with **Heat**, and Cloudsdale letters turn from warm to "concerned" to legal.
- **Heat** (0 to 100) rises when you miss quotas, get spotted, or rescue ponies; it falls slowly over time and when you sabotage Cloudsdale. Heat drives Collector visits: fines first, then a padlocked pen, then a Collector taking an unmarked pony from your ranch at dawn. **Marked ponies cannot be taken.** That makes Blooming the defense.
- **Bloom as rescue (the core loop, inverted).** Everything you learned about care (favorite foods, pen companions, toys, racing) now protects ponies, because a Bloomed pony cannot be drained. The Bloom meter is now a race against Heat.
- **Raids on Intake Corrals** (one per region, built after the reveal; Cloudsdale gathers Drifters there for the lift). At night, slip past Collector lanterns (the stealth from the reveal), and beam ponies out of the corral and into your bag (capture under pressure). Each raid frees ponies and raises Heat.
- **Cutting pylons.** the Corporation's weather pylons stand in each region. Hold the Scoop on a pylon's core at night (a long, struggling beam hold, like a heavy catch) to cut it. Each cut lowers Heat, removes gray rain from that region (fewer new Drifters), and **takes one band out of the Rainbow**. The sky is the progress bar.
- **The Dye Vat** (in Bramble's cellar, the hidden half of the customizer). Repaint a rescued pony's coat and mane so the Corporation's registry cannot find her. It is the character customizer, used on someone else to hide them. Lowers Heat.
- **Sending ponies down the Lantern Line.** Marked, rescued ponies can walk out along the Lantern Line at night, escorted past one Collector post. Each one sent is a permanent rescue (the ending tally). Their postcards arrive later, each in different words.
- **The town joins in.** Help townsfolk with requests to recruit them: Pip carries warnings (early notice of Collector visits), Bramble hides ponies, and Fennel, once you bring him the truth (a real letter from the Works' intake ledger, found on a raid), joins and starts sheltering ponies on his farm.
- **The casino** stays honest. Winning races with a rescued pony is a fast way to Bloom her ("her talent is speed!").

**Rescue climax: Lights Out.** With all four pylons cut, Static can get you back into the Works on Color Day. The player rides the lift up with the town behind them (Fennel, Pip and Bramble, if recruited). Inside, using the walkway route from the reveal:
1. Free the line: beam the waiting ponies out of the queue.
2. Find long-term holding, and Clover (if Fennel was recruited).
3. Reach the control room and **pull the same lever the Factory path pulls**, the other way.

The rainbow goes out. The Works' lights fail floor by floor. For the first time the valley's sky is just sky, and the sunrise in real colors is the most beautiful thing in the game.

## 6. Endings

The ending is chosen at the climax from the player's choices and tallies. Tallies counted after the reveal: `delivered` (ponies sent to the Works), `rescued` (ponies sent down the Lantern Line or Bloomed while under Heat), `allies` (Fennel, Pip, Bramble recruited), and `clover` (rescued or not). Each ending is a short in-engine epilogue (a slow camera over the valley with the final grade, the ponies who are there or are not, and a closing letter), then credits, then the game continues in a post-game state.

| Ending | Condition | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|
| **The Brightest Rainbow** (Factory) | Factory path, Jubilee lever pulled, `rescued` under 5. | The valley is gray and quiet under the most beautiful rainbow ever made. The ranch is enormous and empty. Tumble sits on the porch facing the Works. The last letter is a pension plan. |
| **Gray Gold** (Factory, mixed) | Factory path, Jubilee lever pulled, `rescued` 5 or more (ponies secretly Bloomed or let go while on the Factory path). | The same rainbow, a little thinner. A few marked ponies live wild in the Thornwood that the Company never counted. Cloudsdale audits you: "a small discrepancy." The last letter is a polite investigation. Tumble watches the woods instead of the Works. |
| **Lights Out** (Rescue) | Rescue path, Works lever pulled, `rescued` 20 or more, all three allies, Clover rescued. | No rainbow. Real sunrise. The town in the square, Fennel and Clover together, every rescued pony in your pens and fields. The last letter is from Pip: the first ordinary letter in the game. |
| **Half Light** (Rescue, mixed) | Rescue path, Works lever pulled, but `rescued` under 20, or fewer than three allies, or Clover not rescued. | The rainbow is out, but the valley is half-empty; a missing ally's house is dark, and Fennel's postcards are still on the wall. Bittersweet. The last letter lists the names you could not reach. |
| **Placement** (fail-forward) | Rescue path, Heat reaches 100 and stays there through a collection day. | The player is collected at dawn. No struggle shown: the screen fills with the player's own coat color, then white. Tumble's postcard arrives afterward, in your own hoofwriting. Then credits, and the game offers to load the last autosave from before the final collection day, so this ending is never a softlock. |

**Delivered ponies on the Rescue path** count against the good ending: each one after the reveal lowers `rescued` by one in the Lights Out check. You can always cave for money, and the game remembers.

**Post-game:** after an ending's credits the save continues in that ending's world. Factory: gray, rich, contracts keep coming. Rescue: free, poor, no rainbow, no Collectors, Drifters still roam (and still Bloom). The title screen remembers the endings seen.

## 9. Tone rules

- **Dread over shock.** Never show the process. Show its inputs (a line of silhouettes), its outputs (a rainbow, a dye bottle), and its paperwork.
- **The tone shift is a dial, not a switch.** The `dread` scalar (0 to 1) drives color grade, fog, light, music layers and ambience together. It rises a little in Act Two, jumps at the reveal, then follows the player's path: up with Factory deliveries, down with pylon cuts and rescues.
- **The Company is always polite.** Its copy never threatens, only "notes", "regrets" and "looks forward to". The worst letter in the game thanks you.
- **Ponies are people.** Every pony has a face that reacts. The saddest thing on the Factory path is a pen of ponies who have stopped emoting.
- **Canon Equestria, original plot.** The show's ponies and places appear as themselves; the plot and its core cast are original. The fan song is the inspiration, not a source: none of its lyrics or audio appear.
